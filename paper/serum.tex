\title{\textbf{SERUM}: Content-Aware Agentic Containment of Malware\\
Under an Unobserved Payload}
\author{Amritha S.}
\date{Draft --- \today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Malware propagates only across hosts exploitable by its payload, so the
effective contagion graph is a payload-specific subgraph of the network. We
study the \emph{defender} who does not observe the payload and must infer
\emph{which exploit is spreading}, online, from the vulnerability-consistency
structure of the infected set, then allocate a limited containment budget under
that belief. We show (i) a combinatorial identifiability theory: because the
propagation subgraph is fixed by \emph{observable} node attributes (the CVEs each
host carries), the exploit is identifiable from a saturating outbreak iff the
intersection of the infected hosts' vulnerability profiles is a singleton---a
condition a defender can check in advance from its asset inventory; (ii) a
content-aware agent that plans under a Bayesian belief over the exploit,
optionally warm-started by a CVSS/LLM threat-intel prior; and (iii) an evaluation
on real NVD/CVE data with software-monoculture network zones. Against the
strongest deployable structure-only immunization the content-aware agent reduces
infection by $18.6\%$ relative (a $0.6$-point absolute cut, $1.5\%\!\to\!0.9\%$;
$p=1.1\times10^{-5}$), beats an oracle that receives the signature after a delay,
and is Pareto-dominant on availability and containment speed; on a real
organisational topology the absolute gap widens to $5.9$ points
($17.6\%\!\to\!11.7\%$). We are careful to report absolute magnitudes and
per-trial win rates alongside relative reductions, since the absolute infection
numbers are small. We are explicit about scope: it does not beat an oracle ensemble on
synthetic data, and the gated-propagation model itself is not novel. The central
finding is \emph{content-aware containment on real network structure}: defending
the payload-specific vulnerable subgraph substantially outperforms structure-only
immunization precisely when vulnerable zones diverge from topological hubs (real
org departments: $-28.4\%$, $p{=}1.7\times10^{-7}$), whereas on hub-aligned
synthetic regimes the edge is small. Online exploit inference is a refinement on
top---it matters most under misleading threat intel---not the driver of the win.
Notably, effective containment does \emph{not} require identifying the exploit:
defending the observation-\emph{consistent} vulnerable subgraph suffices, because
confusable exploits share victims---which is precisely why the agent is robust to
partial observation, mis-identification, and adversarial evasion. Formally the
inference is an instance of \emph{online, graph-induced, adversarial group
testing}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A self-propagating worm exploiting a specific vulnerability (CVE) can infect a
neighbouring host only if that host runs the vulnerable software. The network a
worm actually traverses is therefore not the physical topology but the
\emph{vulnerable subgraph} induced by the carriers of its target CVE. A defender
that knows which exploit is loose can defend exactly that subgraph; a defender
that sees only topology wastes budget immunizing hubs the payload cannot use.

The realistic difficulty is that the defender does \emph{not} observe the
payload. It sees who is infected and its own asset inventory. Because
propagation is vulnerability-gated, every host infected \emph{by spread} must
carry the target CVE---so the infected set is itself evidence about the exploit.
SERUM turns this into an online Bayesian inference (a POMDP whose hidden state is
the exploit) and acts under the resulting belief.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We lead with the empirical finding and treat the
inference machinery and theory as its enablers.
\begin{itemize}
\item The central result: a \textbf{content-aware agent} (\S\ref{sec:method},
\S\ref{sec:exp}) that defends the payload-specific vulnerable subgraph and, on a
\emph{real} organisational topology where vulnerable zones diverge from the hubs,
\textbf{substantially outperforms every structure-only baseline}---winning
essentially every paired outbreak ($-28.4\%$, $p=1.7\times10^{-7}$)---while on
hub-aligned regimes the edge is small. It plans under a Bayesian belief with a
payload-conditioned exposed-vulnerable-degree score, switching isolation to
patching as the belief sharpens.
\item A combinatorial \textbf{identifiability characterization} for the
unobserved exploit (\S\ref{sec:theory}) that explains \emph{when} the method helps:
identifiable iff the intersection of infected profiles is a singleton; confusability
is exactly the subset order on carrier sets. The condition is exact by construction;
its non-trivial content is the measured \emph{sample-complexity rate}
(\S\ref{sec:extended}).
\item A \textbf{real-data evaluation} (\S\ref{sec:exp}) on NVD/CVE with
software-monoculture zones, with paired statistics and an honest account of where
content-awareness does and does not help.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Formal framing: online, graph-induced group testing.}
The identifiability condition of \S\ref{sec:theory} is a \emph{separating-system}
condition in the sense of R\'enyi (1961); confusers are cover-free-family
violations (Kautz--Singleton 1964); imperfect inventories give \emph{noisy}
group testing \citep{atia2012boolean}; honeypot canaries
(\S\ref{sec:extended}) make it \emph{adaptive}; multi-exploit payloads are the
\emph{multi-defective} extension \citep{aldridge2019grouptesting}. Each
propagation infection is one positive test whose profile-set is intersected into
the running support (Prop.~\ref{prop:supp}). So the number of infections to
identify inherits the same $\Omega(\log_2 K)$ bit-count that group testing does,
a rate we verify empirically (\S\ref{sec:extended}, median~$5$ infections at
$K{=}30$, $\approx\!1.02\log_2 K$).

\paragraph{What is \emph{not} novel.} The gated-propagation model is a special
case of multitype bond percolation \citep{allard2009multitype} and the premise of
software-diversity worm models; classical worm models already gate on a
vulnerable population \citep{chen2003aawp,staniford2002own}. Learning-based
immunization is established \citep{meirom2021rlgn,fan2020finder}. We claim none of
these. The closest system, CyGym \citep{cygym2025}, has vulnerability-gated spread
and budgeted defense but uses a \emph{static} prior over zero-days. Our lead claim
is \emph{content-awareness on real topology}---defending the inferred vulnerable
subgraph where it diverges from the hubs---not online inference \emph{per se},
which we find buys little over a good static prior (\S\ref{sec:exp}) and matters
mainly under misleading intel.

\section{Problem formulation}\label{sec:formulation}
A host graph $G=(V,E)$; each host $v$ carries a vulnerability profile
$X(v)\subseteq C$ over a CVE universe $C$, with $\carriers(c)=\{v: c\in X(v)\}$
and vulnerable subgraph $G[c]$. A worm has unknown target $c^\star$. Propagation
is gated: an infected $u$ infects a susceptible neighbour $w$ with probability
$\beta$ only if $c^\star\in X(w)$ (SI; SIR with recovery time $r$ is a variant).
Each step the defender observes the infected set (not $c^\star$) and spends a
budget $B$ on \textsc{patch} (immunize a host, availability-preserving),
\textsc{isolate} (remove a host, availability cost), or \textsc{segment} (cut a
link), minimising a multi-objective cost of final infection, availability loss,
and time-to-containment.

\section{Identifiability of the exploit}\label{sec:theory}
Let $\supp(I)=\bigcap_{v\in I}X(v)$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact support]\label{prop:supp}
After observing a propagation-infected set $I$, the CVEs consistent with the
observation are exactly $\supp(I)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Saturation]\label{prop:sat}
Let $R$ be the component of $G[c^\star]$ containing the seeds. As $\beta\to1$ the
outbreak infects exactly $R$, and the observer learns $\supp(R)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Identifiability]\label{thm:id}
$c^\star$ is identifiable from a saturating outbreak over $R$ iff
$\bigcap_{v\in R}X(v)=\{c^\star\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Confusability = subset order]\label{prop:conf}
$c'$ remains consistent after a saturating $c$-outbreak iff $R\subseteq
\carriers(c')$; globally, $\carriers(c)\subseteq\carriers(c')$ implies $c'$ is
confusable with $c$ for every $c$-outbreak.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}
Order CVEs by the subset relation on carrier sets. A CVE is globally identifiable
iff no other CVE's carrier set is a superset of its own.
\end{corollary}

Proofs are elementary set arguments (see supplementary \texttt{docs/THEORY.md}).
The consequence differs from cascade-mixture identifiability
\citep{hoffmann2020mixtures}: the candidate propagation graphs are not latent but
$\{G[c]\}$, fixed by observable profiles, so identifiability is a set-containment
condition a defender can \emph{evaluate in advance}. Because the belief's
surviving support is exactly $\supp(I)$ by construction (Prop.~\ref{prop:supp}),
Theorem~\ref{thm:id} is a \emph{characterization of the observation model}, not an
empirical hypothesis; that our implementation realizes it on $116/116$ real-data
cases is a consistency check on the code, not independent validation. The
substantive, non-tautological content is the \emph{rate} at which infections pin
the exploit (\S\ref{sec:extended}, group-testing bound). On real NVD profiles
about $54\%$ of CVEs are identifiable from a saturating outbreak.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\textbf{Belief.} A posterior over $c^\star$: hard consistency (Prop.~1) for the
theory, and a \emph{soft} likelihood for robustness---each infected host
down-weights rather than eliminates a CVE, so a single false-positive detection
cannot exclude the truth. The prior is prevalence, or a CVSS/LLM
\emph{threat-intel} prior (weaponizability from severity $\times$ exploitability)
that warm-starts cold decisions.

\textbf{Content-aware policy.} Score each frontier host by its belief-weighted
\emph{exposed-vulnerable degree}---the expected number of susceptible,
exploitable neighbours it would infect next---and spend the budget top-down. When
the belief's support collapses, switch from isolation to patching (keep hosts
online). The full-observation oracle and an \emph{oracle-after-delay} (signature
arrives at $t=\delta$) bound the value of inference.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
We build networks with segment-correlated software (connected zones share a
software image; a \texttt{homophily} knob controls monoculture) and draw target
CVEs from a prevalence band per trial. Every policy faces the identical outbreak
(paired design). Real-data profiles come from cleaned NVD records (network attack
vector, no user interaction); per-CVE $\beta$ from CVSS. All real-data results
derive from a single \emph{committed, pinned} NVD snapshot (published
2026-03-21--2026-04-17; \texttt{data/clean/cves.csv} with a \texttt{data\_card.json}),
so they reproduce bit-for-bit rather than depending on a live fetch date.

\paragraph{Findings (synthetic real-CVE profiles).} Against the best fixed
baseline, content-aware reduces infection by $18.6\%$ (paired Wilcoxon
$p=1.1\times10^{-5}$) and beats the per-trial ensemble oracle on real data
($p=2\times10^{-3}$)---because real software zones diverge from topological
centrality. It beats oracle-after-delay: inferring the exploit online beats
waiting for a signature. The advantage scales with outbreak severity and holds
under SIR (recovery). \emph{Honest reading of the magnitudes.} These are
mean-of-paired-trials effects on small absolute infection numbers; the per-trial
picture is heavier-tailed. In the low-prevalence bands the content-aware agent
wins a \emph{minority} of individual outbreaks ($16/40$, $18/40$) while the mean
reduction is carried by a smaller set of large wins, and one band is not
significant ($p=0.14$); the win rate and significance grow with severity (up to
$24/40$, $p=10^{-3}$ in the most severe band). We report win rates alongside means
throughout rather than leading with relative percentages. The threat-intel prior
gives a $\sim37\%$ cold-start reduction under tight budgets and washes out with
evidence.

\paragraph{Real topology (flagship).} On the SNAP \texttt{email-Eu-core} graph
(1004 hosts, 42 real organisational departments used as software zones) with real
NVD CVEs, structure-only immunization barely helps because the vulnerable
department-zones do not coincide with the topological hubs:

Content-aware cuts infection by $28.4\%$ vs the best fixed baseline
($p=1.7\times10^{-7}$) and beats the ensemble oracle ($p=1.9\times10^{-7}$). The
result is significant across BA/WS/RGG topologies (rel.\ reductions
$+17.7/+11.5/+30.2\%$, all $p<10^{-3}$). \emph{Crucially, and unlike the
synthetic-CVE regime where content-aware wins only a minority of individual
outbreaks, on the real topology it wins essentially every paired trial}---$20/20$
vs betweenness \emph{and} vs the ensemble oracle ($p=8.8\times10^{-5}$) in the
multi-topology run (\texttt{results/real/snap\_topologies.json}), and $37/40$ in a
budget-$8$ replication. The lead result is therefore not a heavy-tailed minority
effect; it is where content-awareness matters most. \emph{Variance caveat.} Outbreak
outcomes are highly dispersed---per-arm SD $\approx 0.10$, comparable to the means
and larger than the $5.9$-point effect (content-aware $0.117\pm0.103$, betweenness
$0.176\pm0.101$)---so a given outbreak's outcome swings widely; the small $p$
reflects the paired design (identical outbreak per policy), not separated marginal
distributions. The claim is a consistent \emph{relative} improvement per outbreak,
not a low-variance absolute guarantee.

\paragraph{Head-to-head vs defenders in the spirit of the closest systems.} The
two nearest systems occupy epistemic stances we can reproduce on our substrate, so
we compare against faithful reimplementations of those \emph{stances} (not the full
original systems) on identical paired outbreaks (real NVD, $n{=}500$, $K{=}40$, 40
trials): a \textbf{static-prior defender in the spirit of CyGym} (a static,
common-knowledge exploit prior with \emph{no} online belief update) and a
\textbf{DAVA-style data-aware allocator} (allocation conditioned on the
observed-infected subgraph, but \emph{exploit-blind}). Against the DAVA-style
allocator the content-aware agent wins by $+0.74$pp ($+43.8\%$ rel., paired
Wilcoxon $p=2.8\times10^{-4}$): defending the \emph{inferred} vulnerable subgraph
beats vaccinating the observed-infection frontier---indeed it underperforms even
degree immunization ($1.70\%$ vs $1.52\%$), because vaccinating
exposed-but-non-exploitable hosts wastes budget, which is precisely the thesis.
Against the static-prior defender the edge is smaller but
significant: $+0.19$pp ($+16.6\%$ rel., $p=1.1\times10^{-2}$), and content-aware
wins only $8/40$ \emph{individual} outbreaks---online inference changes the
outcome in a minority of trials (those where the prevalence prior is wrong enough
to matter) and is a wash otherwise, consistent with the belief-freezing ablation
(L2). \emph{Reading.} Content-awareness---defending the exploit-specific
subgraph---is the dominant source of the advantage; online inference of the
exploit adds a small, significant, minority-of-trials refinement on top. Artifact:
\texttt{results/closest\_baselines.json}, baselines: \texttt{serum/baselines/closest.py}.
\emph{Scope (important).} These are reimplementations of each system's
\emph{epistemic stance}, not the systems themselves: the static-prior defender is
our own agent with the belief frozen (an ablation), not CyGym's offline PSRO game;
the DAVA-style allocator is a per-step greedy shield-value proxy, a
\emph{conservative lower bound} on DAVA's dominator-tree strength (a stronger
implementation could narrow the gap). So the honest claim is "content-awareness
beats a static-prior / data-aware defender \emph{of this class}," not "beats CyGym
/ DAVA"; a port of the released systems is the fair follow-up.

\paragraph{Pareto dominance (infection vs availability).} Sweeping the budget
($2$--$12$), content-aware occupies the low-infection, high-availability corner:
at every budget it is simultaneously lower-infection and higher-availability than
every structural baseline (e.g.\ at budget $2$: $3.7\%/99\%$ vs degree
$9.6\%/96\%$). It patches precisely once its belief sharpens; structure-only
defenders must isolate blindly. See \texttt{results/pareto.png}.

\paragraph{Identifiability, as a characterization.} The belief's support equals
the profile intersection by construction, so its agreement with
Theorem~\ref{thm:id} ($116/116$ over real-data networks) confirms the
implementation is faithful, not that an empirical prediction held; $\sim54\%$ of
real CVEs are identifiable from a saturating outbreak, the rest having confusers
(Prop.~\ref{prop:conf}). The testable claim is the \emph{rate} (below).

\paragraph{Robustness to inference-evasion (Stackelberg attacker).} An attacker
that picks a non-identifiable (confusable) payload to defeat the belief does not
help itself: the content-aware edge is $+22.4\%$ under an evasive attacker vs
$+17.7\%$ under a random one. This follows from Prop.~\ref{prop:conf}: a
confusable exploit's victims are a subset of its confusers', so the hedged belief
still defends the true victims. Inference-evasion is not a winning strategy.

\paragraph{Active sensing.} Honeypot value-of-information probing captures the
payload almost immediately (step $\approx1$), enabling early
availability-preserving patching: availability rises ($99.2\%$ vs $98.4\%$) at no
infection cost. Consistent with the evasion result, passive inference already
suffices for containment, so sensing is an identification/availability tool.

\paragraph{Learned policy.} A cross-entropy-method policy over belief-augmented
features matches the hand-designed content-aware agent on held-out data
($1.09\%$ vs $1.04\%$) and beats degree/betweenness ($1.65\%/2.11\%$); it
independently assigns the largest weights to the belief-conditioned features,
validating the design. This is learning \emph{under exploit uncertainty}, not
vanilla GNN-RL immunization.

\paragraph{Honest scope.} On \emph{synthetic} data content-aware does not beat an
oracle ensemble that cherry-picks the best structural heuristic per outbreak
($p\approx0.84$); and on near-universal (``popular'') exploits over
geometric/small-world topologies structure-only can win, since almost every host
is vulnerable. Content-awareness helps when the exploit is stealthy enough that
its victims are a distinctive, mislocated-from-the-hubs zone.

\section{Related work}
A full, source-verified survey is in the supplementary \texttt{docs/LITERATURE\_REVIEW.md};
we summarise the five neighbouring literatures and our position in each.

\paragraph{Propagation model (not novel).} Vulnerability-gated spread is a
special case of heterogeneous multitype bond percolation
\citep{allard2009multitype}, itself a case of the SIR-percolation mapping
\citep{newman2002spread}; the security-side antecedent is software-diversity
worm spread via graph colouring \citep{odonnell2004diversity}, and classical
worm models gate on a vulnerable population \citep{staniford2002own,chen2003aawp}.
The same attribute-gated-subgraph pattern underlies lateral-movement models
\citep{powell2019lateral}. We claim no novelty at the mechanism level.

\paragraph{Containment (our baselines).} Structure-only immunization is mature:
degree/acquaintance \citep{pastorsatorras2002immunization,cohen2003acquaintance},
spectral node/edge removal (NetShield) \citep{chen2016netshield}, collective
influence \citep{morone2015influence}, and connectivity-preserving link
containment \citep{matamalas2018containment}. The nearest ``data-aware'' method,
DAVA \citep{zhang2015dava}, conditions on the \emph{observed infected} subgraph;
SERUM conditions on the \emph{inferred exploit} (a sharper conditioning) and adds
an availability objective.

\paragraph{Inference \& identifiability (the core neighbourhood).} Source
detection \citep{shah2011rumors} infers \emph{where} an outbreak began; NetInf
\citep{gomez2010netinf} infers the \emph{graph}; SERUM infers \emph{which
exploit}. The nearest inference-from-shape is SCENARIOID \citep{scenarioid2023},
an \emph{offline, feature-engineered} classification of intervention regimes with
no identifiability guarantee; we do online belief tracking tied to an exact
condition. \emph{We are candid that this delta is partly a matter of setting}:
online-vs-offline is an operational choice, and our exploit-identification task
(recover a categorical label under a hard consistency observation model) is
arguably \emph{easier} than SCENARIOID's scenario classification from partial
cascades---it is security-relevant and admits an exact condition, not a harder
inference problem. Likewise, our identifiability characterization's nearest
neighbour is cascade-mixture identifiability \citep{hoffmann2020mixtures}, whose
condition is on \emph{latent} edge structure; ours is on \emph{observable} node
attributes and holds online per cascade---but that observability is precisely what
makes our problem tractable, so this is a different (and in a sense easier)
setting, not a strictly stronger theorem. Contrast also mechanism discrimination
\citep{cencetti2023contagion} and parameter identifiability
\citep{kiss2023identifiability}.

\paragraph{Cyber-defense decision-making.} Belief-state POMDP defense is
established \citep{miehling2015pomdp,miehling2018pomdp}, as is deception to infer
attacker \emph{intent} \citep{shinde2020deception}; SERUM's hidden state is the
\emph{exploit}, sensing is a payload-capturing honeypot, and the machinery is
active hypothesis testing \citep{naghshvar2013active} / value of information
\citep{leskovec2007celf}. Our adversary evades the defender's \emph{inference}
(cf.\ estimation-evasion \citep{shokri2012location,fanti2015spy}), not a detector
\citep{biggio2013evasion}. \textbf{The closest system is CyGym}
\citep{cygym2025}: it has vulnerability-gated spread and per-action costs but a
\emph{static, common-knowledge prior over zero-days} and an offline equilibrium
policy \emph{independent of the realised exploit} --- no online belief update and
no sensing. That online-inference gap is what SERUM fills.

\paragraph{ML/LLM components (deliberately standard).} GNN-RL graph intervention
\citep{meirom2021rlgn2,fan2020finder} and LLM-as-Bayesian-prior
\citep{yan2025llmpriors} are established; LLM CVE reasoning is benchmarked
\citep{ctibench2024} and paralleled by population-level exploitation priors
(EPSS) \citep{epss2021}. SERUM's learned policy is ``learning under exploit
uncertainty'' (belief-augmented features), and its LLM layer is a grounded prior
over a fixed CVE candidate set, corrected online by the Bayesian tracker.

\section{Limitations}
We state these plainly; several are load-bearing.

\textbf{(L1) The favorable regime is not validated on real host-level data.}
Content-awareness helps only when vulnerable \emph{zones diverge from network
hubs} (\S\ref{sec:exp}); on hub-dominated graphs structure-only immunization
already suffices. Whether real enterprise networks satisfy the favorable
condition is an empirical question we \emph{cannot} answer with public data: the
join of a real segmented topology with real per-host vulnerability inventories
exists only as proprietary scan data (e.g.\ enterprise Tenable/Nessus exports),
restricted by privacy. Our two real topologies are imperfect proxies---email-Eu-core
is a communication graph, and the Autonomous-Systems graph's nodes are
organisations, not hosts. Validating (or refuting) applicability on a real
host-level segmented network with measured vulnerabilities is the single most
important piece of future work.

\textbf{(L2) Online inference is a refinement, not the driver.} Ablations
(\S\ref{sec:exp}) show containment succeeds without identifying the exploit:
defending the observation-\emph{consistent} vulnerable subgraph over-covers the
true victims (Prop.~\ref{prop:conf}), so freezing the belief at its prior costs
only $0.1$--$0.2$ points. The identifiability theory (\S\ref{sec:theory}) is thus
a \emph{characterisation} of when full identification is possible and the
group-testing bridge---not the mechanism behind the empirical win.

\textbf{(L3) Semi-synthetic profiles.} CVE catalog and topology are real, but the
host$\leftrightarrow$CVE assignment is \emph{modeled} (NVD product popularity plus
software-monoculture zones), not measured.

\textbf{(L4) Infection-detection noise (addressed).} Earlier drafts assumed
perfect infection observability. We now model an imperfect sensor on infection
status (missed detections + persistent false alarms; \S\ref{sec:extended},
``Detection-noise robustness''). Content-awareness degrades \emph{gracefully}:
the paired edge over structure-only stays non-negative across a realistic
sensor-noise grid, with the false-alarm channel---which poisons the belief but
not a belief-free heuristic---the sensitive one. It is a graceful-degradation
result, not a significant win under noise; per-point tests at $n{=}20$ are
mostly underpowered.

\textbf{(L5) Adaptive adversary (addressed, with a bounded caveat).} Beyond the
naive poisoner we now evaluate a white-box attacker that \emph{best-responds to
the robust agent's audit rule} (\S\ref{sec:extended}, ``Adaptive adversary'').
The RobustAgent holds against it up to a large poisoning budget ($6\%$ of the
fleet); the attacker gains only a small, single-point, Holm-uncorrected edge at
an extreme $10\%$-of-fleet poisoning. This bounds the poison-robustness claim
honestly: the audit is not unbreakable, but breaching it requires a poisoning
budget larger than the defender's own. A fully adaptive adversary that also
best-responds on payload \emph{and} timing jointly (not only decoy placement)
remains future work.

\textbf{(L6) Multiplicity (partly addressed).} The $11$ headline paired
comparisons were corrected family-wise (\S\ref{sec:extended}, "Multiplicity"):
all $11/11$ survive Holm-Bonferroni at $\alpha{=}0.05$ and $9/11$ under the
stricter Bonferroni; five remain at $p_\text{adj}\!<\!10^{-3}$. This corrects
multiplicity \emph{within} the chosen family; it does \emph{not} address the
garden of forking paths across the dozens of experiments run, nor the researcher
degree of freedom in choosing which $11$ to headline. No post-hoc correction
fixes that---only pre-registration would, which we did not do.

\textbf{(L7) Abstract model.} Propagation is a probability over an exploitability
graph; there is no real malware, packet-level emulation, or scanning/C2 layer.

\paragraph{Responsible use.} SERUM is defensive---it studies faster,
lower-collateral containment---and contains no weaponizable attack code.

\section{Extended results and honest negatives}\label{sec:extended}
\emph{The main-body contribution is the four core claims (the identifiability
characterization of \S\ref{sec:theory} and the content-aware containment results
of \S\ref{sec:exp}); this appendix collects supporting breadth, scope-bounding
experiments, and honest negatives.}

Since the flagship containment experiments, we have measured the theory's group-testing
rate on real data, characterised the ambiguity structure, extended to polymorphic
payloads, addressed poisoning robustness, added value-weighted objectives, and
instantiated the framework on IoT-botnet containment. Each subsection is one
paragraph plus, where the number is a curve, one small table. Every table is
regenerated from a single \texttt{results/*.json} artifact, and the numbers here
are cross-checked in \texttt{tests/test\_paper\_claims.py}.

\paragraph{Sample complexity of identification.}
The information-theoretic bit-bound of adaptive group testing with one defective
in $K$ items is $\lceil\log_2 K\rceil$. Empirically, on 12 real-NVD networks
($n{=}400$, $K{=}30$, homophily $0.4$, 76 identifiable-CVE trials), the belief
support collapses to a single CVE after a \textbf{median of 5 propagation
infections} --- $1.25\%$ of the fleet, $p90{=}2.25\%$, $18.5\%$ of the reachable
vulnerable component. Median $\text{hosts}/\log_2 K\approx 1.02$: the empirical
median lands essentially on the bit-bound despite profile correlations. All $76$
theoretically identifiable CVEs identified in practice ($100\%$; verifies
Thm.~\ref{thm:id}). Artifact: \texttt{results/sample\_complexity.json}, script
\texttt{scripts/sample\_complexity.py}. \emph{Caveat.} On a $K{=}16$ Zipf
synthetic the ratio is $\approx\!2.0\log_2 K$; real product-based profiles are
more informative than the toy.

\paragraph{Confusability decays with $K$.}
We measure per-CVE confuser counts and identifiable fractions as $K$ grows on
real NVD-derived networks ($n{=}400$, homophily $0.4$, 8 nets, 240 live CVEs at
$K{=}30$). Flagship at $K{=}30$: operational identifiable fraction $50.8\%$,
global $63.7\%$ (weakly larger, as it must be: operational $\Rightarrow$ global;
cross-checked as a theorem in the test suite). Median confuser count is $0$;
p90 is $3$ (operational). Sweep:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
\toprule
$K$ & live CVEs & op.\ ident. & global ident. \\
\midrule
10 & 10.0 & 0.675 & 0.700 \\
20 & 20.0 & 0.500 & 0.550 \\
30 & 30.0 & 0.358 & 0.400 \\
50 & 50.0 & 0.345 & 0.465 \\
80 & 80.0 & 0.200 & 0.384 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Identifiable fraction decays monotonically in $K$ --- more CVEs mean more
subset-order dominators. Artifact: \texttt{results/confusability.\{json,png\}}.

\paragraph{Polymorphic (multi-exploit) payloads.}
A polymorphic worm carries a set $S\subseteq C$ and infects any host whose
profile intersects $S$ (the OR-of-exploits payload). The posterior support after
observing infected set $I$ becomes the family of size-$|S|$ hitting sets
$\{S: \forall v\!\in\!I,\ X(v)\cap S\neq\emptyset\}$: the multi-defective
analogue of Prop.~\ref{prop:supp}. On real NVD ($n{=}300$, $K{=}16$)
identifiable fraction decays $77.8\%{\to}49.6\%{\to}38.2\%{\to}27.1\%$ for
$k{=}1{\to}4$; $k{=}2$ needs a \textbf{median of 18 propagation infections} to
pin the hitting set (vs $5$ for single-CVE), a ratio of $2.61$ to the bit-bound
$\log_2\binom{16}{2}\!=\!6.9$ --- profile correlation compounds with $k$.
Artifact: \texttt{results/multi\_exploit.\{json,png\}}. \emph{Scope.} Belief
conditions on knowing $|S|$; end-to-end containment for polymorphic payloads is
future work --- this closes the identifiability layer only.

\paragraph{Diversity-for-observability: canary planning.}
Given a canary budget $B$, greedily insert canary hosts to \emph{engineer}
identifiability. Adding a host only grows each $\carriers(c)$, so no subset-order
witness can be destroyed: greedy singleton canaries are regret-free with respect
to the identifiable count. On $8$ real-NVD nets ($K{=}30$, $n{=}400$) greedy
reaches $100\%$ global identifiability at \textbf{median $B^\star=15$} canaries
(exactly $K_\text{live}-I_0^\text{global}$, matching the singleton upper bound)
vs $B^\star\!\geq\!60=2K$ for random (coupon-collector), a $4\times$ ratio.
Greedy wins $8/8$ trials at every $B>0$ with $\Delta\text{CI95}>0$. Artifact:
\texttt{results/diversity.\{json,png\}}. \emph{Scope.} Singleton greedy is not
provably minimum-canary-optimal (a multi-CVE canary can pin two CVEs when
their dominators are disjoint); a set-cover LP is the natural follow-up.

\paragraph{Optimal stopping is trivially T{=}0 (honest negative).}
A defender who waits $T$ steps before acting still folds the propagation into its
belief. If waiting sharpens the belief enough to place patches better, the
wait-vs-spread curve should be U-shaped. It is not. Sweeping fixed and adaptive
stops on $24$ paired real-NVD trials ($K{=}30$, $n{=}400$, budget $5$):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrr}
\toprule
$T$ & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 5 & 8 & 12 \\
\midrule
hedge  & 0.020 & 0.033 & 0.048 & 0.065 & 0.090 & 0.124 & 0.155 \\
commit & 0.021 & 0.031 & 0.047 & 0.062 & 0.089 & 0.124 & 0.153 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Per-trial oracle-best $T$ is $T{=}0$ in $24/24$ (hedge) and $22/24$ (commit; the
two exceptions win by $\leq 1$pp at $T{=}1$). Every adaptive rule has strictly
positive CI95 gap versus the per-trial oracle. \emph{Reading.} With a perishable
per-step budget and a hedged/inferring defender, optimal stopping collapses to
``act immediately.'' Stopping would matter under cumulative budgets, an
activation cost, or a pathological prior --- none apply here. Artifact:
\texttt{results/optimal\_stopping.\{json,png\}}.

\paragraph{Poison-robust defender.}
A committee of diverse belief-agents does not defeat belief-poisoning attacks
(belief members fail in correlated ways). The fix is \emph{detection~+~hedging}:
\texttt{RobustAgent} measures the fraction of new propagation-infected hosts
that carry its current MAP CVE and moves a trust weight $\alpha\!\in\![0,1]$
toward it, scoring hosts as
$\alpha\cdot\text{content} + (1-\alpha)\cdot\text{structural}$. Poisoning is
one-shot; the real worm keeps revealing the truth, so $\alpha$ falls under
attack. On the poison-decoys sweep it tracks \emph{the better} of
\{content-aware, degree\} at every level: $1.13\%$ clean (vs degree $1.71\%$),
$1.49\%$ at $15$ decoys (beats both), $1.77\%$ at $30$ decoys (vs single-soft's
collapse to $4.94\%$). Script: \texttt{scripts/robust.py}. \emph{Honest framing.}
This is \emph{graceful degradation, not a robustness win}: under poisoning the
content-aware advantage largely evaporates and the robust agent's value is that it
falls back to---never below---structure-only defense. The contribution is a safety
net that prevents belief-poisoning from making content-awareness \emph{worse} than
a payload-blind heuristic, not a method that keeps winning under attack.

\paragraph{Adaptive adversary: best-responding to the audit (SR5).}
The poison-robustness above was tested against a \emph{naive} poisoner, inviting
the objection that it is a strawman. We therefore build the white-box attacker
that \emph{best-responds to the trust audit itself}. Since real spread is gated
on $c^\star$, the audit's pass rate for a poisoned MAP $c'$ converges to the
carrier overlap $|\carriers(c^\star)\cap\carriers(c')|/|\carriers(c^\star)|$: a
naive poisoner picks a disjoint $c'$, so overlap $\to 0$, $\alpha$ collapses, and
the agent falls back to structure. The best response instead maximises
$\text{overlap}\cdot|\carriers(c^\star)\setminus\carriers(c')|$ --- keeping the
audit passing while still leaving part of the true victim set undefended (a pure
superset $c'$ over-covers the truth by Prop.~\ref{prop:conf} and is useless, so
the attacker is forced onto this knife-edge). On real NVD ($n{=}500$, $K{=}40$,
budget $5$, 60 paired trials/point) this attack has genuine teeth --- it drives a
single audit-free soft belief to $2.5$--$4.5\%$ infection --- yet
\textbf{RobustAgent holds against it up to $30$ decoys ($6\%$ of the fleet)}: its
paired gap over the structure-only floor is not significant there
($p\!\geq\!0.056$). The attacker's edge grows monotonically with the poisoning
budget and becomes marginally significant only at an extreme $50$ decoys ($10\%$
of the fleet: $+0.42$pp, uncorrected $p=0.022$) --- and that single grid point
does \emph{not} survive Holm correction across the seven budgets
($0.022\times7\approx0.15$). So the audit is not unbreakable, but breaching it
costs a poisoning budget \emph{twice} the defender's own containment budget; the
robust agent degrades gracefully rather than collapsing. Artifact:
\texttt{results/adaptive\_attack.json}, attacker: \texttt{serum/attack/adaptive.py}.

\paragraph{Cost \& blast-radius: value-weighted steering.}
Real fleets are lopsided: a small tail (DBs, DCs, gateways) matters far more
than the rest. We add heavy-tailed \texttt{value} and \texttt{cost\_isolate} on
hosts (Pareto tail, $\alpha{=}1.2$), a \texttt{blast\_radius} metric (fraction
of total value ever infected), and a \texttt{value\_weighted} flag on the
content-aware agent (score neighbours by \texttt{value}). Paired enterprise
sweep (30 trials, $n{=}500$, \texttt{value\_max}$=100$): value-steering
\textbf{cuts blast\_radius by $-1.00$pp} (CI95 $[-1.82,-0.19]$, $18/30$ wins)
while raising \texttt{infected\_fraction} by $+0.83$pp --- an honest trade, not
a Pareto win. Effect vanishes at \texttt{value\_max}$=20$ (a caveat, not a
fudge: real fleets are skewed). Artifact:
\texttt{results/blast\_radius.json}.

\paragraph{IoT-botnet application (Mirai-style).}
Instantiate SERUM on a device-type firmware monoculture
(camera/dvr/router/thermostat/doorbell/light/hub/printer);
\texttt{mirai\_payload} targets default telnet credentials (spans
camera/DVR/router/hub); host \texttt{value}~$=$~uplink Mbps so
\texttt{blast\_radius}~$=$~DDoS capacity conscripted. Paired sweep (20 trials,
$n{=}600$, RGG, budget $3$): content-aware cuts DDoS blast \textbf{$-8.71$pp
vs degree} (CI95 $[-11.13,-6.47]$, $20/20$ wins) at higher availability
($99.5\%$ vs $91.25\%$), Pareto-dominating here because the belief narrows fast
enough to switch from isolation to patching. Value-steering adds a marginal
$-2.09$pp on blast (CI95 $[-3.98,-0.17]$, $12/20$). Absolute infection is high
($60\%+$): Mirai-style payloads on tight budgets \emph{do} form botnets; the
deltas are what to read. Artifact: \texttt{results/iot\_botnet.json},
scenario: \texttt{serum/scenarios/iot.py}, doc:
\texttt{docs/APPLICATION\_IOT.md}.

\paragraph{Is the advantage an artifact of the monoculture knob?}
A fair worry: the favorable regime (vulnerable zones misaligned from hubs) is
produced by the software-monoculture \texttt{homophily} parameter we set, so the
win could be manufactured. We test this by sweeping homophily from $0$ (software
assigned independently of zones---no monoculture) to $0.8$ (real NVD, $n{=}500$,
40 paired outbreaks). The content-aware edge over the best structural baseline is
\emph{significant at every level, including homophily $0$} ($+0.26$pp,
$p=6.5\times10^{-4}$), and is \emph{non-monotonic}, peaking mid-range
($+0.60$pp at homophily $0.2$). So the advantage is \textbf{not} an artifact of
the monoculture assumption: homophily controls only how spatially \emph{clustered}
the vulnerable set is; content-awareness helps whenever that set diverges from the
topological hubs, which holds even when it is scattered. \emph{This rebuts the
monoculture-\emph{knob} artifact worry, not the semi-synthetic-assignment
limitation:} even at homophily $0$ the host$\leftrightarrow$CVE mapping is a
popularity \emph{model}, not a measured inventory, so L1/L3 stand in full.
Artifact: \texttt{results/homophily\_sensitivity.json}.

\paragraph{When is online inference load-bearing?}
The head-to-head against CyGym-static (a static prior with no online update) shows
online inference buys only $+0.19$pp under a \emph{good} (prevalence) prior --
content-awareness, not the online update, is the dominant advantage (consistent
with the belief-freezing ablation, L2). Online inference earns its keep when the
prior is \emph{wrong}: giving both defenders a misleading prior (mass on the
loudest, most-prevalent CVE, which is usually not the one loose), the
content-aware agent's edge over the frozen-prior defender roughly \textbf{doubles
to $+0.44$pp} ($p=1.8\times10^{-2}$, 40 paired real-NVD outbreaks) because it
corrects the prior online while the static defender stays committed to the wrong
subgraph. \emph{This misleading prior is deliberately constructed (a worst-case
intel regime), so this is not evidence online inference is generally important} --
under a good prior it is nearly inert. The effect is real but modest even in this
adversarial case, so we do not claim online inference as the driver of the
containment win; it is a refinement that matters most under bad threat intel.
Artifact: \texttt{results/inference\_value.json}.

\paragraph{Detection-noise robustness.}
Beyond inventory noise on host \emph{vulnerabilities}, the defender's sensor on
\emph{infection status} is imperfect: EDR misses dwelling implants (false
negatives), and stuck telemetry flags idle hosts as compromised (false
positives). We add a two-channel model---\texttt{detection\_miss} (a real
infection permanently absent from the observed set) and \texttt{detection\_false}
(a susceptible host persistently reported infected)---so that both the belief
update and the computed frontier see the corrupted view, while ground truth is
retained for scoring. On real NVD networks ($n{=}400$, $K{=}30$, budget $5$, 20
paired trials per point) the paired content-aware-minus-degree infection gap
stays \emph{positive at 9 of 10 noise points}, including the entire
missed-detection channel (miss $\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$, gaps
$+1.18/+1.18/+0.41$pp)---missed detections withhold evidence symmetrically from
the belief and the structural frontier, so they do not preferentially hurt the
inferring defender. The one non-positive point is the pure false-alarm channel at
$2\%$ ($-0.09$pp, a tie): false alarms \emph{poison the belief}
(Prop.~\ref{prop:supp}'s consistency evidence is corrupted) in a way they cannot
poison a belief-free heuristic. The effect does not compound---at $5\%$/$10\%$
false alarms the gap is positive again as both policies degrade together. This is
\textbf{graceful degradation, not a new significant win} (per-point Wilcoxon at
$n{=}20$ reaches $p<0.05$ only at the noiseless and miss$=0.1$ points); the
false-alarm channel is the one to monitor. Artifact:
\texttt{results/detection\_noise.json}, script:
\texttt{scripts/detection\_noise.py}.

\paragraph{Multiplicity: family-wise-corrected p-values.}
We report $11$ headline paired-Wilcoxon comparisons across seven experiments
(synthetic-CVE flagship, both SNAP topologies, BA/WS/RGG synthetic sweeps, and
two adversarial regimes). The Holm-Bonferroni step-down procedure at $\alpha{=}
0.05$ rejects \textbf{all $11/11$}; the more conservative Bonferroni rejects
$9/11$ (only the two marginal SNAP autonomous-systems comparisons drop). The
largest Holm-adjusted $p$ among the surviving comparisons is $p_\text{adj}{=}
0.042$ (SNAP-AS vs ensemble oracle). Five of the eleven remain below
$p_\text{adj}{<}10^{-3}$ after correction, including both the synthetic-CVE
and SNAP email-Eu-core flagship comparisons against the best-fixed baseline;
the BA/WS synthetic-topology and adversarial-identifiable comparisons land at
$p_\text{adj}\!\in\![10^{-3},10^{-2}]$, still comfortably rejecting. Artifact:
\texttt{results/multiplicity.json}, script: \texttt{scripts/multiplicity.py}.
\emph{Scope.} The family excludes clear duplicates (identical seeds/spec
across scripts) and only includes p-values already backing headline claims;
per-experiment secondary comparisons (e.g.\ value-steering deltas) are not
aggregated here.
